## Plan (agent output, reviewed by the human before implementation)

### Context

You want 3D packing for **physical loading**, as a module of our own. The earlier measurements say why the
`bin-packing` crate is not enough: its fast algorithm (DeepestBottomLeftFill) left 21–32 of 196 boxes with
nothing underneath, its `Auto` took 3.3 s for the same 4 bins, and it has no support, weight, stacking,
unloading-order or balance logic. So we write a solver in which stability and the load rules are hard
constraints and the result is reproducible by construction. It has the shape of `packages/gdrr2bp-wasm`
(Rust → WASM, committed glue and base64 payload, typed TS wrapper), but the code is ours.

Your answers: all three situations (parts in a crate or carton, cartons on pallets, elements in a truck or
container); version 1 enforces weight and stacking limits, orientation locks, unloading order and load
balance; bins are a list of types with a cost. Stable support is always on.

Outcome: `loaded = make.pack3d(shapes, { bins: [...] })` returns copies of the shapes, moved and turned into
place, one sub-group per bin with the bin outline, and a report at `modeler.stats` (as `pack()` does). The
WASM is fetched and instantiated only by a script that calls `pack3d`, the way the IFC classifier is.

### Decisions I took (veto any at approval)

1. **Names:** `make.pack3d`, package `packages/pack3d-wasm` = `@archiyou/pack3d-wasm`, crate `pack3d-wasm`, TS class `Packer3D`. License Apache-2.0 like core (no vendored code, so no ATTRIBUTION entry; papers are cited in the README).
2. **Stability is static:** at least `support` (default 0.8; pallets want 1) of an item's base rests on the floor or on item tops, **and** its centre lies inside the convex hull of that contact area. No braking/sliding stability, no dunnage or strapping (documented limit).
3. **Items are their axis-aligned bounding boxes as modelled**, z up; weight is uniform, so the centre of gravity is the box centre. Bins are boxes (x = length, y = width, z = height, x = 0 at the front wall).
4. **Hard vs best effort:** geometry, support, orientation, weight, stacking and unloading order are hard: a returned plan never violates them (an independent validator runs inside `solve`, and a violation is an error, never a silent plan). **Load balance is optimised and always reported, not guaranteed:** if the limits cannot be met the bin says `balanceOk: false` with the measured numbers and `make.pack3d` warns.
5. **Integers only** in all rules (mm, grams; costs are the only floats and are only added and compared); rounding is conservative (item dims and weights up, bin dims and limits down), as `pack()` does with `unitsUp/unitsDown` (Make.ts:54).
6. **Budget by iterations and a work counter, never the clock**; `seed` default 0, same lesson as the GDRR change.

### Design

#### Wire model (JSON in, JSON out, like gdrr2bp's `solve(instanceJson, configJson)`)

- **Item type** (identical items merged, with `count`): `dims [x,y,z]`, `weight`, `orientations` (6-bit mask over the axis permutations; presets `fixed` = as modelled, `upright` = as modelled or turned 90° about z, `free` = all 6; custom = which axes may point up, with or without turning), `maxLoad` (weight that may rest on it, absent = unlimited, 0 = nothing on top), `stop` (unloading stop, lower is unloaded first, 0 = no order).
- **Bin type:** inner `dims`, `maxWeight`, `cost`, `stock` (absent = unlimited), `door` (`+x` `-x` `+y` `-y`, absent = unloaded from the top only), `balance` (centre-of-gravity tolerance as a fraction of length/width from the centre; optional two supports along the length with load limits for axle loads, split by the lever rule).
- **Config:** `seed`, `iterations`, `maxEvaluations` (hard cap on work), `support`, `gap` (clearance between items, applied like `kerf`).
- **Output:** per bin the type, the placements `{item, copy, x, y, z, orientation}` and a load report (weight, fill, centre of gravity, axle loads, `balanceOk`); `unplaced`; stats.

#### Rules (exact integer geometry, one function each, all re-checked by the validator)

| Rule | Test |
|---|---|
| Inside bin, no overlap | box bounds; strict AABB overlap against placed items |
| Orientation | candidate orientation must be in the item's mask (duplicates collapsed when dims are equal) |
| Support | contact area = overlap of the base with tops at exactly its z (floor = full); `area·1000 ≥ support·base` |
| Centre of gravity | doubled centre coordinates inside the convex hull of the contact rectangle corners (integer cross products, boundary inclusive) |
| Bin weight | sum + item ≤ `maxWeight` |
| Stacking | the item's weight is shared over its supporters by contact area (rounded up) and passes on down through theirs; every item's carried load ≤ its `maxLoad` |
| Unloading order | for every pair with different stops, the item delivered earlier is not blocked by the later one: not below it, and not between it and the door (overlapping cross-section along the door axis) |
| Balance | at bin close: centre of gravity within tolerance, axle loads within limits (i128 cross-multiplication, no division) |

#### Constructive decoder (per sequence of items)

- **Candidates:** extreme points of the placed items (Crainic, Perboli & Tadei 2008: corner points projected along the other axes until they touch an item or wall) with a residual-space prefilter; each candidate gets the exact checks above, cheapest first (bounds → residual space → collision → support → weight → stacking → order).
- **Choice:** candidates are visited in a fill order (`layers`: z, x, y for pallets and crates; `walls`: x, z, y for trucks); first feasible per orientation, then the orientation/position with the best score (most contact area with walls and neighbours, lowest height, balance pull for heavy items).
- **Bins:** first fit over open bins; a new bin takes the cheapest type per volume that holds the item and has stock; afterwards each bin tries cheaper types for its own content ("right-sizing"). Out of stock → the rest is `unplaced`, never dropped silently.
- **Balance:** a construction bias (heavy items toward the target centre of gravity) plus a repair step: translate the whole bin content along the free length/width, which keeps every other rule intact.
- **Portfolio:** both fill orders × a few sorts (stop descending, then volume, base area, weight); best objective wins. No search needed to get a valid plan.

#### Improvement search (LAHC, deterministic)

Genes: order (permuted within stop groups only), orientation choice per item, fill order. Moves: swap, relocate, change orientation, pull an item out of the least-filled bin to the front. Objective, lexicographic: unplaced items → bin cost → balance violation → sum of squared bin fills (empties bins to eliminate them) → used length. Seed from `PortableRng` (xoshiro256++ via splitmix64: copy `packages/gdrr2bp-wasm/src/util/rng.rs` with its known-answer tests). Rule for the whole crate: no `HashMap`/`HashSet` iteration, no float math beyond `+ − × <`, explicit tie-breakers in every sort.

#### Lazy loading (follows the IFC precedent exactly)

- `Make.ts`: module-level lazy singleton like `loadIFCModule` (Modeler.ts:56–63): `import('@archiyou/pack3d-wasm')` then `new Packer3D().init()`. Only `import type` from the package elsewhere. `Make.loadPack3d()` for direct Modeler use. `make.pack3d()` throws the `explainIFC()`-style message ("Scripts get it automatically; when calling the Modeler directly, await make.loadPack3d() first") if it is not loaded.
- `Runner._loadPack3dWhenUsed(request)`: copy of `_loadIFCWhenUsed` (Runner.ts:924) with `/\bpack3d\s*\(/` over the script **and** its components; called next to `_loadIFCWhenUsed` (Runner.ts:881) and in `_executeLocalInScriptStatements` (after `_prepareModules`, ~:1334). A call hidden from the scan (computed names) fails with the clear error: the same accepted trade-off as `ModuleRegistry.referencesGlobal`.
- `buildscripts/check-pack.ts`: add the package to `MUST_BE_REAL_DEPS` (:94) and a `LAZY_ONLY` entry (:83) whose marker is a string only the wrapper contains, so the publish check proves the chunk is not in the eager graph.
- The existing 2D packer is loaded eagerly today (`Make.setArchiyou()` and `packReady()` in Runner.ts:1096/:1329). Moving it to the same mechanism is a ~20-line follow-up (trigger `/\b(pack|boarding)\s*\(/`) and deliberately **not** part of this plan.

#### Script API (TypeBox schema `Pack3DOptionsSchema` in `schemas.ts`, TSDoc with the three situations as examples)

```js
// cartons on a pallet
loaded = make.pack3d(cartons, {
  bins: [{ name: 'pallet', length: 1200, width: 800, height: 1800, maxWeight: 1000, cost: 25 }],
  support: 1,
  maxLoad: (c) => c.name().startsWith('glass') ? 0 : 150,   // kg that may rest on it
})
// elements in a trailer, two stops, unloaded at the rear, with axle limits
loaded = make.pack3d(walls, {
  bins: [{ name: 'trailer', length: 13600, width: 2450, height: 2700, maxWeight: 24000, door: '+x',
           balance: { cog: { x: 0.15, y: 0.05 }, axles: [{ at: 1800, maxLoad: 9000 }, { at: 11600, maxLoad: 20000 }] } }],
  rotation: 'free',
  stop: (w, i) => i < 6 ? 2 : 1,
})
```

Options: `bins` (list; one entry = one fixed type), `rotation` (`'upright'` default | `'fixed'` | `'free'` | `(shape, i) =>` any of those or `{ up, yaw }`), `weight` (kg or function; default `shape.weight()`, shapeAnnotations.ts:168, 0 with one warning when missing), `maxLoad`, `stop`, `support`, `gap`, `seed`, `maxIterations`. Model units convert through `MM_PER_UNIT` (units/UnitConverter.ts:25). Result group `loaded` with `bin1…` (outline + placed copies, side by side along x as in `pack()`); shapes the solver could not place are removed from the scene and listed in `modeler.stats.unplaced` with a warning. Each of the 6 orientations maps to a rotation of the copy (identity, `rotateZ(90)`, `rotateX(90)`, `rotateY(90)` and two compositions); a property test proves the rotated bbox equals the placed extents.

### Verification

- **Validator as oracle:** independent O(n²) re-check of every hard rule; `solve` returns an error instead of a plan that fails it. `cargo test` runs ≥ 5 000 seeded random instances over all rule combinations with zero violations, plus unit tests per rule where each has a control (a floating item, a heavy item on a fragile one, a locked item that does get turned when free, a blocked earlier stop) and a golden layout.
- **Reproducibility:** same seed, same bytes; other seeds valid and sometimes different; **native == WASM byte-identical** on the fixture corpus (12/12 was the bar for GDRR), asserted in vitest against hashes generated natively, so it runs without Rust in CI.
- **Quality:** benchmark families (crate parts, pallet cartons, trailer elements, the 196-box job) report bins, fill %, ms per phase. Reference points: the crate's DBLF/EP (unconstrained, so an optimistic bar) and, if you download them, the OR-Library container-loading files `thpack1–9` (no license statement on the page, so not vendored). Gates are fixed from the Phase 1 numbers and recorded; each later phase must not make any family worse. Targets to confirm: decode of 200 items ≤ 10 ms native; default solve ≤ 1 s in WASM.
- **TS:** `pnpm --filter @archiyou/core exec vitest run tests/unit/make tests/unit/runner/runner.pack3d.lazy.test.ts`, then `pnpm --filter @archiyou/core test:runner` (what CI runs). Tests: rotation property test for all 6 orientations, no overlap and inside the bin on the real meshes, weights from materials, unplaced reporting, errors. Lazy tests: a script without `pack3d` leaves the module unloaded, one with it inside a component works.
- **Gates:** `typecheck:budget` (no new errors), `tsc --noEmit` in the new package, `pnpm --filter @archiyou/core build && … check:pack` (chunk lazy, dependency real).
- **End to end:** `pnpm -s archiyou run` on three scripts (crate, pallet, trailer) to read the stats; optional look at the layout in the editor.

### Risks and limits to accept

- **Scope:** four rule families and three situations in version 1 is a lot; the phase gates keep each rule verifiable, and balance is the one that is best effort.
- **Quality ceiling:** extreme points plus LAHC is mid-field. If the families show a gap, layer/block building for identical cartons is the next step (not planned now).
- **Realism:** AABB items, uniform density, static stability only; non-box parts waste their corners.
- **Size of inputs:** a few hundred items per call; the work cap makes larger inputs finish with a valid, less tight plan instead of hanging.
- **Payload drift:** CI has no Rust, so a stale base64 payload is only caught by the golden test through WASM.
- **Toolchain:** Rust 1.94, wasm-pack 0.14; dependencies limited to wasm-bindgen, serde, serde_json.

### Phase 2: weight, stacking and unloading order

Each rule went in as validator check → failing test → decoder → control. 17 validator tests (each rule found, each
with a control that passes), 58 unit tests, 16 behaviour tests, the fuzz widened with weights, load limits, stops,
doors and weight limits: 200 instances in debug and 5 000 in release (4.9 s), zero violations.

What the work settled:
- **Loads.** A box passes its weight and what it carries to the boxes under it, shared by contact area and rounded up
  to the gram. The validator recomputes this from the finished plan, top down; the decoder keeps it up to date as it
  places boxes with the same function, so the two agree to the gram. While loads are tracked a box is never slipped in
  under one that is already placed (its weight would have been shared out without it). Rounding up is monotone, which
  a largest-remainder split is not (adding a gram could take one away from a box).
- **Doors at the low end.** The first run of the unloading test put every item in its own bin: with the door at −x
  the solver filled from the door end, so each later stop blocked the earlier ones. Bins with a door at −x or −y are now
  decoded mirrored and flipped back, and a door on y adds a third fill order that runs its walls along y.

Bench (all gates of Phase 1 unchanged: 1 / 5 / 3 / 2 bins at both support values). New families, with the rules laid over the
pallet job (weights at 250 kg/m³, 600 kg per pallet, every fifth type fragile, every fifth+2 able to carry 30 kg) and over the
trailer (500 kg/m³, 24 t, door at +x, walls for stop 2, floors and roofs for stop 1, 3 t and 1 t load limits):

| Family | Boxes | Support | Bins | Fill % | ms | Heaviest bin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pallet-rules | 196 | 0.8 / 1.0 | 8 / 8 | 37.1 | 59 / 56 | 323 kg |
| trailer-stops | 31 | 0.8 / 1.0 | 2 / 2 | 32.8 | 2.2 / 1.9 | 22.8 t / 21.6 t |

(the validator). **Target for Phase 4:** pallet-rules 6 bins.

### Phase 3: balance, and which bin to open

71 unit tests (the balance arithmetic checked by hand: bands, the lever rule, exact shifts to the millimetre), 21 behaviour tests,
the fuzz now with centre-of-gravity bands and supports on random bins: 200 instances in debug, 5 000 in release (10.8 s), zero
violations. The validator recomputes the weight, centre of gravity, support loads and the verdict of every bin and compares them with
what the plan reports, so a report cannot be wrong without being found. A balance that is not met is not a violation: the plan says so.

What the work settled:
- **Moving the load is free.** Every requirement on the balance is an interval of the doubled moment, and moving a load by `s` changes it
  by `2 s W`, so the shift that meets it is an integer division. The load is moved the least that works, within the room it has; bins
  decoded mirrored (door at the low end) are shifted the other way round.
- **First fit did not know about balance**, so the plan with two balanced bins was never generated: two boxes of 8 t and 1 t in a 2 m
  bin cannot be balanced together (1.6 m of load, 0.4 m of room), one each they can. A piece now does not go into an open bin when it
  would make a balance impossible that is possible now; if that opens a new bin, so be it. Without a balance nothing changes.
- **Balance outranks cost** in the objective (unplaced, then unbalanced bins, then cost), a deviation from the plan, where cost came
  first: a plan that is unsafe is worse than a dearer one.
- **Which bin to open.** By cost per volume, which is right for many pieces (ten 400 cubes: two big bins for 16, not ten small ones for
  30); the last bins are moved into a cheaper kind afterwards. Right-sizing only swaps one bin for one, so two cubes stayed in the big
  bin (8) although two small bins cost 6: the portfolio now also tries opening the cheapest kind, when there is a choice of kinds.

Bench: all earlier gates unchanged (1 / 5 / 3 / 2, pallet-rules 8, trailer-stops 2). New family trailer-balance (trailer-stops with a
centre of gravity within 15 % of the middle along the length and 5 % across, supports at 1.8 m and 11.6 m carrying at most 9 t and
20 t): 2 bins, both balanced, 2.4 ms.

**Gate added:** trailer-balance 2 bins, none unbalanced.
